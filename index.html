Abstract

Assessing a recorded presentation against a rubric requires evidence from audio, video, and slides. Expert labels are costly to obtain, experts who apply the same rubric disagree, and general multimodal language models concentrate their scores near the middle of the scale.

We present AIMPACT, a multimodal assessment system that scores a presentation in three stages. An evidence-decoupled multi-agent stage converts the three input streams into a shared, traceable evidence record, from which specialist agents score the rubric dimensions. A reliability-guided collaboration graph then estimates the reliability of each dimension’s evidence without labels, activates deliberation only where a disagreeing peer rests on more reliable evidence, and combines the outcome into a council prior. STAR-PO, a rubric-conditioned generative judge built on Qwen3-8B, aligns this prior with expert scoring preferences. It is first fine-tuned on expert corrections to the prior and then optimized on policy under a reward that scores agreement with the confidence-weighted expert distribution together with evidence faithfulness, confidence calibration, and label-free rubric following.

On a corpus of 35 classroom presentations annotated by five experts on eight dimensions, the full system reaches a Spearman correlation of 0.833 with the expert consensus, against 0.374 for a transcript-only language-model baseline, and each stage improves the axis it targets. Under matched inputs, AIMPACT ranks presentations more like a human expert than four omni multimodal judges, which compress their scores toward the middle of the scale. Because the rubric is an input, the trained judge transfers to the speech dimensions of SpeechOcean762 without retraining.

Method

AIMPACT separates what was observed from how it should be judged, keeping every assessment traceable to evidence.

Evidence extraction

Frozen perception models and deterministic operators organize video, audio, and slides into a shared temporal evidence memory.

Reliable collaboration

Specialist agents score rubric dimensions while a reliability-guided graph activates deliberation only where better evidence warrants it.

Expert preference alignment

STAR-PO turns the council prior into rubric-conditioned scores, confidence, rationales, and time-anchored coaching feedback.

AIMPACT reframes automated presentation assessment as a transparent, evidence-grounded collaboration between specialized agents and an expert-aligned judge. By preserving provenance, reasoning over reliability, and treating the rubric as an input, the system delivers adaptable scores and specific coaching feedback while keeping human judgment at the center.
