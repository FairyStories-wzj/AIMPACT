Abstract

Recorded presentation assessment needs evidence from speech, video, and slides, but expert labels are expensive and general multimodal models often flatten scores. AIMPACT builds a traceable evidence record, resolves disagreements through reliability-guided collaboration, and uses the rubric-conditioned STAR-PO judge to produce expert-aligned scores and coaching feedback. On 35 classroom presentations, it reaches a Spearman correlation of 0.833 with expert consensus, compared with 0.374 for a transcript-only baseline.

Conclusion

AIMPACT provides a transparent framework for assessing recorded presentations from speech, video, and slides. By organizing multimodal evidence, coordinating specialized evaluators, and aligning judgments with expert preferences, it produces traceable scores and practical coaching feedback across rubric dimensions. The results suggest that presentation assessment can be both reliable and interpretable without losing the context behind each judgment.
